REGION & LOCALE

Nothing is hard-coded to India.

Every mock shows one persona: Mihir, home in Bengaluru, paying in rupees, travelling to Vietnam. That's a sample user, not the product. Each value in those screens comes from one of four places, and the app renders it for whoever is looking.

THE USER
Home and preferences
Home country and city, currency, number and date formats, units, 12/24-hour clock, language, settle-up handles, passport country (optional).
THE TRIP
What the crew agreed
Destinations, dates and time zones, the currency the trip settles in (organiser's home currency by default, changeable).
THE DESTINATION
Facts about the place
Local currency, emergency numbers, ride and payment apps, plug type, entry rules, booking partners that work there.
THE APP STORE
Price and tax
Plus and Trip Pass prices, currency and sales tax come from the user's store country. We set price tiers per country; the store does the rest.

How we know where "home" is

  1. Phone settings first. Region, currency, units, clock and first day of the week are read from the device on first launch (Expo's localization module exposes all of these).
  2. Phone number if they sign in by phone: its country code confirms the home country.
  3. Confirmed in onboarding. The style screen already shows "Home: Bengaluru · ₹ INR · Change". One tap fixes a wrong guess.
  4. Location by network only as a last hint, never to override what the person chose.
  5. Always editable in Profile › Home & region. Moving countries changes the display, never past trips' records.

Crews from different countries

  • A trip settles in one currency, picked when it's created (default: organiser's home currency).
  • Each expense stores its original amount and currency plus the rate on the day it was logged.
  • Everyone sees the settle currency; if their own home currency differs, it shows underneath in small type.
  • Settle-up offers the payment app both people share: UPI between two Indian accounts, Venmo between two US ones, PayPal or Wise across borders, and always "Mark as paid".
  • Dates, times and units always follow the viewer, so one crew can see "21 Nov, 13:05" and "Nov 21, 1:05 PM" on the same expense.

Rules for the code

  • Money is stored as whole minor units plus an ISO currency code, never as a formatted string.
  • Every number, date, time and unit goes through the platform's locale formatters, never hand-built.
  • All copy lives in translation files; no "₹", "+91", "UPI" or "km" typed into screens.
  • A build check fails if those literals appear in UI code.
  • Destination facts (emergency numbers, plugs, ride apps, currency) live in one table per country, kept in BigQuery with the place index.
  • Payment apps are plug-ins: each one knows its country, its link format and whether it works across borders.

How we test it

Four test personas go through every screen before release, each with a real phone set to their region:
  • Mihir, Bengaluru: ₹, lakh grouping, UPI, km, 12-hour
  • Jake, Austin: $, Venmo, miles, °F, 12-hour, week starts Sunday
  • Emma, London: £, bank transfer, miles, °C, 24-hour
  • Lena, Berlin: €, comma decimals, PayPal, km, 24-hour
Plus one mixed crew (Mihir and Emma on the same trip) to check the two-currency view. The "Trip money by home country" board next to this shows all four.
