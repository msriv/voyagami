How free turns into paid

1. Wall at the moment of intent
Plans for a 3rd trip, a second best order, the first ₫ expense, 10 days before flying, the 6th booking. Never on a random screen.
2. Show the value, then the wall
Work out the answer before asking: "Best order would save 47 min today." "Ha Long has weak signal on the boats." People pay for a number, not a feature list.
3. Let them taste it once
One free best order per trip, a 10-second HD preview of the recap. Having had it makes going without feel like a loss.
4. One payer unlocks the trip
Trip features (best order, multi-currency, live map, bookings) unlock for everyone on a trip when any member has Plus or a pass. The organiser pays happily, and three friends see Plus working.
5. Meters, not surprises
"4 of 5 bookings" sits quietly in trip settings and on the import screen, so the wall never feels like a trap.
6. Loss-framed, honest trial end
Day 6 shows what they actually used ("3 cities offline, 2 h saved on routes") and exactly what changes on free. No fake countdowns.
7. Seasonal and win-back offers
Push yearly Plus before Diwali, summer and December holidays. Lapsed trialists get 30% off their first year, once.
What this should do to the numbers. With gates similar to Wanderlog's and Splitwise's, aim for the base-to-good rows below (3–4.5% on Plus) instead of the cautious one. Adapty's benchmark puts the median 5–9 day trial at ~45% trial-to-paid for card-required trials, so the job of the free plan is to get people to start a trial at a moment they need it. Treat both as targets to test, not promises.

What V1 got wrong, and what changed

The ₹299 crew pass undercut everythingA crew of four paid ₹75 each for a whole trip. Anyone taking fewer than 13 trips a year was better off never subscribing. Now the pass is per traveller and priced by trip length, so the price grows with the trip.
The monthly plan had the same holePeople use a travel app in bursts. At ₹149 a month you subscribe for the trip month and cancel, which beats any pass. Monthly is gone; the Trip Pass is the "just this trip" option.
The break-even math skipped GSTIndian store prices include 18% GST. V1 counted ₹88 kept per subscriber per month; the real figure was ₹74, so the "6% must pay" target was really over 7%.
The ladder now holds: 3 Week passes (₹1,047) cost more than a year of Plus (₹999). Travel once a year: buy a pass. Travel three or more times: Plus is cheaper and covers you on everyone's trips. The upgrade credit turns happy pass buyers into subscribers instead of competing with them.

How it's built

  • App Store and Google Play subscriptions with a 7-day free-trial offer; Trip Pass as a one-time purchase per traveller, tier picked from the trip's dates.
  • RevenueCat to handle receipts, trial state and entitlements on both stores (free at small revenue, then a small % — confirm current terms).
  • Entitlement stored on the user (Plus) or on the traveller + trip (Trip Pass); the Go API checks it before metered calls.
  • Day-5 reminder sent by our own scheduler + FCM + email, not left to the store.
  • Region pricing set per store country, so India sees ₹ and others see local equivalents.
